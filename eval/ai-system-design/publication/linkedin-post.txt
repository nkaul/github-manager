One of the most useful changes we made to GitHub Manager was giving the AI workflow a receipt.

A repository could have Topics and Tags, a completed analysis, and still have no Project. Treating “no Project” as “not analyzed” blurred the workflow and made repeat inference too easy.

We separated four things: accepted labels, analysis completion, freshness, and pending review.

That led to five lessons I’d carry into another AI product:

• Preserve purpose, not just keywords. “Uses FastAPI” and “belongs to this initiative” are different claims. Negation and relationship evidence matter.

• Retrieve a shortlist before asking for a decision. Our baseline uses sparse canonical-candidate retrieval; dense embeddings and reranking remain experiments to justify with evidence.

• Keep authority outside the model. AI proposes existing concept IDs. The application validates them, and accepted or rejected user decisions stay protected.

• Remember completed work. A valid NO_MATCH is a completed result. Previously analyzed repositories can reconcile locally; fresh re-analysis remains explicit.

• Account for each call. Count the actual request, including schema overhead. Reserve a conservative maximum. Keep uncertain usage reserved. Resetting app data does not refill the budget.

A real 20-case regression round produced 50 correct assignments, no unexpected assignments and two misses: 98.0% micro F1.

That set contained 8 public and 12 synthetic repositories and was reused after policy clarification. Broader independent validation is still needed before generalizing the result.

The next questions are about better evidence selection, stronger evaluation and when another model call adds value.
